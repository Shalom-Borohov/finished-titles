# כותרים: design

A personal iPhone PWA that records every anime, series, movie, book, game and comic its owner has finished or stopped, plus games the owner keeps playing (87). This file is the source of truth for product behaviour. New behaviour is added as a new numbered decision: "(added YYYY-MM-DD, extends/clarifies N)".

## Decisions

### What gets recorded

1. **Types are anime, series, movies, books, games, comics and Other.** Why: these are what the owner finishes. Documentaries count as movies or series and audiobooks as books. Other covers anything else.
2. **Status is finished or stopped.** There is no want-to-watch or in-progress status. Why: the owner logs once, when something ends, so there is nothing to keep up to date. Games are the exception (87) (amended 2026-11-19).
87. **A game can also be "ממשיך לשחק", for games that never end.** Only games offer this third status. Other types keep finished and stopped. It has no reason or stopped-at season. The rating asks "איך זה?". The poster label reads "ממשיך" and the poster isn't dimmed. A list shows a "ממשיך N" status pill only while it holds an ongoing title, and if that filter is chosen when the last one is finished or stopped, the list goes back to all, as with ratings and genres (75). Stats add "N ממשיך" to the status counts only when there is one. Changing a form's type away from game turns ongoing into finished, and saving or restoring normalises an ongoing non-game the same way (5). Why: some games get weekly updates or are endless, so neither finished nor stopped is true. It's still logged once, when the owner decides it belongs in the list. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-19 (added 2026-11-19, issue #29, amends 2; extends 16 and 20).
88. **An ongoing game's date means since when.** Its heading is "מתי התחלתי?". It's stored as the same date, defaults to today, and can be a year or blank like any other (8). Ongoing games sort and chart by it like every entry. Switching a game's status to or from ongoing on the form resets the date to today, so a start date never becomes a finish date by accident, or the reverse. Changing an ongoing game's type away from game does the same, because its status becomes finished (87). Why: logging right away needs no extra tap, and the game shows at the top of the list. The status usually changes on the day the game is finished or stopped, so today is the likely date, and it stays editable. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-19 (added 2026-11-19, issue #29, extends 8).
89. **A game can record hours played, a whole number of 1 or more, in any status.** A "שעות משחק" row sits where seasons-watched sits for shows, under the rating. It takes digits only, up to five, and shows "—" when empty. Typing 0 or clearing it saves no hours. Other types never keep hours, and saving normalises them away (5). Why: the owner wants to see how much time a game took. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-19 (added 2026-11-19, issue #28, extends 5).
3. **One entry per show.** A finished series or anime has an optional seasons-watched number. A new season updates the existing entry. Why: the grid stays free of near-duplicates. The cost is one rating for the whole show.
4. **Any type can be stopped.** Stopped entries have an optional reason in the owner's words. Series and anime also have an optional stopped-at season. Other types can mention where they stopped in the reason. Why: abandoned books and games are part of the history too, without extra fields to fill in.
5. **Season fields apply only where they fit.** Seasons-watched is kept only for finished series and anime, stopped-at season only for stopped series and anime, and the reason only for stopped entries. Saving normalises away anything else, so changing the type or status leaves no stale values.
6. **Ratings are words, and optional:** אהוב במיוחד, אהבתי מאוד, אהבתי, היה בסדר, לא אהבתי. Why: the owner asked for how it felt rather than a score, with a way to mark true favourites. Optional so a half-remembered title never blocks logging.
7. **The note is optional free text.**
8. **The finish date is an exact date, a year, or blank.** It defaults to today, and future dates are not allowed, except a saved one kept on edit (95) (amended 2026-11-26). Why: right after finishing, no extra tap is needed; for old titles, blank is more honest than an invented year.
61. **A year-only date has − and + buttons beside the year, and the year can still be typed.** + stops at this year, − stops at 1000 (the lowest four-digit year), and both buttons are off while the typed text isn't a four-digit year. They look like the season stepper. Why: a recent year is one or two taps, while titles from long ago, which year-only is mostly for, would need dozens of taps without typing. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-12 (added 2026-11-12, issue #10, extends 8).
9. **A title is required, and nothing else is.** Why: PRODUCT principle "Never blocked". Type and status always have a value.
74. **Any entry can have genres, ticked from the app's own list of 21 Hebrew genres; they're optional.** The list is אימה, אנימציה, אקשן, דרמה, היסטוריה, הרפתקאות, ילדים, מדע בדיוני, מוזיקה, מותחן, מלחמה, מסתורין, מערבון, משפחה, פוליטיקה, פנטזיה, פשע, קומדיה, ריאליטי, רומנטיקה and תיעודי. These are TMDB's genres in Hebrew, with TV's combined genres split (Action & Adventure into אקשן and הרפתקאות, Sci-Fi & Fantasy into מדע בדיוני and פנטזיה, War & Politics into מלחמה and פוליטיקה), and without TV Movie, News, Soap and Talk. Picking a TMDB title from search fills them in (77), and they stay editable. Titles from other sources are tagged by hand. On the form, a "ז׳אנרים" row sits under the title card and the "פרטים נוספים" row, above the status. The duplicate-title hint (38) stays right under the title card, above the genre row, because it is about the title just typed. It shows the chosen genres on one line, or "לא נבחרו". Tapping it opens all 21 as pills inside the same card, with the chosen ones violet, and tapping it again folds them away. This was picked on the decision page over a separate checklist page and an always-open block, so the form stays short and nothing leaves the form. Genres that were never touched save nothing, so saving an edit leaves the stored genres as they are, including ones the Settings fill (76) saved while the form was open. Unticking all of them saves an empty list, which counts as decided. Genres are kept whatever the type or status, because they describe the title. They're saved as the owner's own record, like title and year (27). TMDB's terms (§1.C) say not to "Cache, for longer than 6 months, any information obtained through or from TMDB or the TMDB APIs". The owner keeps genres anyway, in the app's own words and on the phone only. Why: one consistent set across every type, so one genre finds movies and series together. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-15 (added 2026-11-15, issue #21, extends 9, 10 and 27; narrows 66, whose other details are still never saved).

### Finding and adding titles

10. **Search fills in the poster, year and type, with manual entry always available.** Search covers anime, series, movies, books and games. Comics and Other are manual. Why: search coverage for comics is uneven, and a search failure must never block logging.
11. **Anime, series and movies come from TMDB, books from Open Library, and games from IGDB.** TV animation from Japan is suggested as anime; everything else keeps TMDB's movie or series type. The type stays editable on the form.
12. **AniList is not used.** Its terms prohibit competing list or tracker services, with no personal-use exception found. TMDB also gives one record per show, which matches decision 3 with no season grouping. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06. This replaces the idea of grouping AniList's per-season records.
13. **Searching for a logged title opens the existing entry.** Results already in the list are marked "כבר ברשימה". A match is the same source and source ID. A search result also matches a title added by hand with the same type, title and release year; a title added by hand with no year or another year doesn't mark any result, so another title with the same name can still be added from search. Titles added in the app by hand have no year (40), so in practice only one restored from an older or hand-edited backup can mark a result, and picking a result with the same name as a hand-added title shows no hint. A manual entry being typed matches on type plus title (38). Titles match ignoring case and surrounding spaces. Why: this is how decision 3 stays true. Matching search results by name alone marked both "Dune" films as the hand-added one, so the second could only be added by hand, without a poster (changed 2026-11-23, decided by the owner on 2026-11-22, issue #38).
14. **There is no rewatch count and no bulk backlog mode.** Old titles go through the normal add flow, decided by the owner.
36. **A TMDB source ID includes its media type,** such as `movie/1396` and `tv/1396`. Why: TMDB numbers movies and shows separately, so the bare number can name two different titles (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 13).
38. **A manual entry matching an existing title shows a hint with a way to open it, and can still be saved.** Why: different titles can share a name, and PRODUCT principle "Never blocked". The hint isn't shown when editing (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 13).
39. **New entries start as finished, with the current list's type, or movie outside a type list.** Why: most logging happens when something ends (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 9).
40. **The release year from search shows on the form. Manual entries have no poster or year field.** Why: comics and Other stay quick to log (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 10).
41. **Search covers every source wherever it was opened,** in the order TMDB, Open Library, IGDB, keeping at most 8, 5 and 5 results. Why: the type stays editable, and a wrong list must not hide a title (added 2026-11-07, extends 25; the caps and the order are replaced by 80 and 81).
42. **Until game search is on, the Games list still opens search,** with manual entry one tap away, so nothing changes in the flow once it's on (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 29; game search is on since 78).
43. **Search queries are 2 to 100 characters.** The field stops at 100. Why: a basic limit on upstream calls (added 2026-11-07, extends 25).
80. **Search keeps loading results as you scroll, until every source runs out.** Each page holds up to 20 results per source, which is TMDB's page size. The next page loads when the end of the results comes into view, also when a page doesn't fill the screen, and is added below the results already shown, which never move. The Worker takes `GET /search?q=...&page=<n>`, where `page` runs from 1 to 500 and defaults to 1, and anything else gets 400. It answers `{ results, unavailable, hasMore }`, where `hasMore` is true when any source that answered has another page, and is always false on page 500, so the app never asks for a page the Worker refuses. A source that has run out is still asked and answers nothing. An older app ignores `hasMore` and gets the first page. `unavailable` covers every page loaded so far, and a result already shown isn't shown twice. Why: at most 13 results stopped the owner from finding titles further down (issue #25). Decided by the owner on 2026-11-16, over one bigger page of 20 per source (added 2026-11-16, issue #25, replaces the caps in 41, extends 25 and 57).
81. **Each page shows the current list's type first, then mixes the sources by rank:** the best result of TMDB, Open Library and IGDB, then the second of each, and so on. From Home, which has no type, a page is only mixed. The type must match exactly, so from the Series list a TMDB show suggested as anime isn't lifted. The order applies to each page as it arrives, so from the Games list, games from page 2 come after page 1's other results. The app orders the results, and the Worker never learns which list search was opened from. Why: in source order, a game sat after up to 40 shows and books. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-16 (added 2026-11-16, issues #20 and #25, replaces the order in 41).
82. **The manual entry button sits right under the search field, above the results.** Why: with results loading as you scroll, a button after the last result could no longer be reached, and decision 10 keeps manual entry always available. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-16 (added 2026-11-16, issue #25, clarifies 10).
84. **While the next page loads, "טוען עוד…" shows after the last result. If it fails, "לא הצלחנו לטעון עוד" shows with a "נסו שוב" button, and the results already shown stay.** Nothing marks the end of the results. Why: a failed page must not lose what was found, and a retry is one tap (added 2026-11-16, issue #25, extends 24 and 80).
85. **A later page that comes back without a source that answered the page before it counts as failed,** even though the Worker answers it: its results show, and "נסו שוב" asks for the same page again, where results already shown aren't repeated. A source that was already missing from the page before doesn't count, so IGDB without credentials (79) never stops the paging. If the retry still lacks the source, paging goes on without it, and if it comes back and drops out again, that page counts as failed again. Why: the Worker's `hasMore` covers only the sources that answered, so a source failing midway would otherwise look like the end of the results, or quietly skip its page (added 2026-11-16, issue #25, code review, clarifies 80 and 84).
86. **While more pages are coming, a page with no results says only that some sources are missing, if any are,** and "לא נמצאו תוצאות" or "search is unavailable" (48) wait until there's nothing more to load. Until then the manual entry button keeps "לא מצאתי, הוספה ידנית", and reads "הוספה ידנית" only once search is unavailable under 48, so the button and the message line agree. Why: TMDB's first page can hold only people, which search leaves out, and the next page may hold the title (added 2026-11-16, issue #25, code review, clarifies 48 and 80).
92. **Search's messages and the details page's loading and failure lines are announced by VoiceOver as they change:** "חיפוש…", nothing found, search unavailable, sources missing (48, 86), "טוען עוד…" and its failure (84), and on the details page (66) the failure, "אין פרטים נוספים" and the loading after "לנסות שוב". Each sits in a status line that is on the screen before its text changes, so the change is read out without moving focus. The details page opens with its loading line already showing and focus on the back button, so that first line may not be read out. Why: WCAG 4.1.3 asks for status messages to be announced; without it, a VoiceOver user typing a title heard nothing (added 2026-11-23, issue #34).
61. **Picking a TMDB series or anime from search fills in seasons watched with the seasons aired so far.** The count covers regular seasons, not Specials, whose first episode aired on or before today. The form opens at once, and the number appears when the lookup returns, with no loading indicator. It fills the field only while it's empty, so a number the owner already set stays. It stays editable. It's kept in the form whatever the status or type, so switching to stopped or to a non-show type and back shows it again, and decision 5 drops it when a stopped or non-show entry is saved. The stopped-at season is never filled in. Only a new entry from a TMDB show is looked up: edits, entries opened from "כבר ברשימה", and other types and sources aren't. If the lookup fails or finds no aired season, the field stays empty with no message. Why: finishing a show usually means watching every season aired, so it saves a tap per season. Anime is included even though TMDB splits some anime by arc or cour, because a wrong number is a tap or two to fix. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-12 (added 2026-11-12, extends 3 and 10).
64. **Picking a TMDB series or anime that has ended also records the year it ended, and the form shows the years as a range, such as 2008–2013.** It arrives with the season lookup of decision 61 (seasons watched from search) and is saved on the entry. A series that is still airing shows its start year only. The range shows only when both years are known and differ, and reads left to right like other numbers. Only new picks are looked up, as in that decision, so titles logged earlier keep the start year alone. The end year is kept for series and anime whatever the status, because it describes the show, not how far the owner watched; other types drop it on save, as decision 5 does for seasons. Why: the owner asked for the end year the way the season count arrives (issue #16). Decided by the owner on 2026-11-13 (added 2026-11-13, issue #16, extends 40 and 61 on seasons watched from search).
66. **A TMDB series, anime or movie has a "פרטים נוספים" row under its title on the form, which opens a details page inside the same sheet.** It works for new picks and for entries already in the list. The row follows the TMDB source, not the type chosen on the form, so a TMDB title whose type was changed still has it. The page shows the poster, title, type and years, and the original title when it differs. Below them, wherever TMDB has the data:
    - where to watch it in Israel on a subscription service, with "נתוני צפייה: JustWatch" at the bottom of that panel whenever this row shows;
    - the network, and the next episode's date;
    - the episode count and episode length, or a movie's running time;
    - the creators or director, and the first five cast members;
    - the genres, in Hebrew; a genre the app has no Hebrew name for shows TMDB's English name, so a genre TMDB adds later isn't hidden.

    Rows and panels with no data are left out. The details are looked up each time the page opens and never saved. While they load, the page says "טוען פרטים…"; a failure says "הפרטים לא זמינים כרגע" with a retry button; no data at all says "אין פרטים נוספים". A next episode date that isn't a real date leaves out just that row. Opening the page moves focus to its back button, and Back returns to the form exactly as it was, with focus on the "פרטים נוספים" row (WCAG 2.4.3, focus order). There is no plot summary and no TMDB score. Why: the owner wanted more about a title without crowding the form (issue #17). The details aren't saved because TMDB limits caching (27) and next episodes and services change over time. TMDB requires JustWatch's credit wherever its watch data shows. Genre names are mapped to Hebrew in the app, because TMDB doesn't document Hebrew genre names. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-13 on the decision page (added 2026-11-13, issue #17, extends 10 and 18).

### Screens

15. **The app opens on Home: one tile per type, with its count and up to three recent posters.** Other is a slim row beneath them. Under the name, a line reads "מה שראיתי, קראתי ושיחקתי" with the total. Why: the owner chose to see the whole collection at a glance, across every medium.
16. **Tapping a type opens its list:** a three-column poster grid, newest first, with search and filters for all, finished and stopped. Stopped titles stay in the grid, dimmed, with their stopped label. Entries are sorted by finish date, newest first, with year-only dates after exact dates of that year, blank dates last, and later-added entries first on a tie.
17. **A list can search inside its type, and Home opens an all-titles list.** A search field on Home, or tapping the total, opens the same poster grid across every type, newest first. Why: the owner may not remember a title's type and wants to see what they finished lately across everything. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-07, replacing per-type search only.
91. **A list's search ignores accents, case, spaces, hyphens, punctuation and symbols such as +, on both the query and the titles,** so "shogun" finds "Shōgun", "spiderman" finds "Spider-Man" and "love death robots" finds "Love, Death + Robots". The Hebrew ׳ and ״ count as punctuation too, so they match a plain apostrophe or quote. Only the search folds text this way: titles show as written, and decision 13's match stays on case and surrounding spaces. Why: the owner types titles quickly on the phone, without accents or punctuation (added 2026-11-23, issue #42, extends 17).
18. **Tapping a poster opens the same form as adding, with a delete button set apart from the rest.** Deleting asks for confirmation.
19. **The tabs are בית, סטטיסטיקה and הגדרות.**
20. **Stats show:** totals by type, titles per year, the rating breakdown, and finished versus stopped. The chart shows the six most recent years that have titles. Older and undated titles are stated beneath it. They count everywhere except the chart (reworded 2026-11-07: the rating and status counts should cover every title).
90. **Stats show the total hours played across games, "N שעות משחק" with thousands separators ("שעת משחק אחת" for exactly one),** on a line under the status counts, only when some game has hours. The hours aren't on the poster label. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-19 (added 2026-11-19, issue #28, extends 20 and 89).
21. **Settings hold:** backup export and restore, the TMDB attribution (decision 28), and the version.
51. **A Home tile fans its three most recent titles, and a title with no poster shows its coloured placeholder.** A type with fewer titles shows only those, and an empty type shows none. Why: comics and Other have no posters, and empty cards read as content that isn't there (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 15).
52. **The add and edit sheet closes with Cancel or Save, not by swiping down or tapping outside it.** Why: a stray gesture mustn't lose what was typed. Open gap: check this feels right on an iPhone (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 18).
53. **A poster's label shows the seasons watched only from two up.** Long labels stay on one line and truncate. Why: a single season says little in a small grid, and it was what got cut off (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 3 and 16).
44. **The search field on Home opens the all-titles list without focusing its search,** so typing takes a second tap. Why: iOS commonly won't open the keyboard after a route change. Open gap: check this on an iPhone, and focus it if iOS allows (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 17).
60. **Settings has "דיווח על באג / הצעה", which opens a sheet to write a bug report or an idea and then opens it as a GitHub issue on this repo,** labelled `bug` or `feature`. The open button stays off until there is text. The title is the first line, up to 80 characters, and the body adds the browser and the version with its build time. The link is at most 4,300 characters, prayer-log's limit. It measured that GitHub keeps a signed-out user's way back to the report only up to about 4,400. A longer report is cut at a whole character and marked "… (הדיווח נחתך כאן)" in the issue, with the device details kept after the mark, and the sheet notes that the rest can be copied from it. The title is cut at whole characters too. GitHub opens outside the app. The sheet then offers to close and says to tap again if GitHub didn't open, and like the add sheet it closes only with its button (52). No list data is sent. Why: it's the same feedback path as the sibling apps (prayer-log, social-log, workout-log). It needs no token or write endpoint, which the fakeable Origin check (59) couldn't protect. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (added 2026-11-10, extends 21).
68. **Settings show the version with its build time, "גרסה 1.0.0 · עודכנה 14.11.2026, 11:05",** in the phone's time zone. Why: the version number never changes, so it couldn't show whether the phone had the latest update. The line is the same as in the sibling apps (added 2026-11-15, issue #19, extends 21).
73. **A list can be filtered by one rating word at a time, together with the status filter and search.** A row under the status pills holds two menu pills, rating first and then genre (75). The rating pill reads "כל הדירוגים" and opens the iPhone's own menu of rating words. Once a word is chosen, the pill turns violet and shows that word, and choosing "כל הדירוגים" clears it. It matches that exact word, with no "and above", because ratings are words, not a score (PRODUCT principle "Your words, not a score"). Stopped titles match by their rating too. Only words that some title in this list has are offered. A menu pill with nothing to offer is hidden, and the row is hidden when both are, since an empty menu has nothing to choose. Why: the owner asked to find, for example, every אהוב במיוחד (issue #21). The menu pills were picked on the decision page over pill rows and a filter sheet, because they add one row and keep the posters high. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-15 (added 2026-11-15, issue #21, extends 16 and 17).
75. **A list can be filtered by one genre at a time (74), the same way as by rating (73).** Its pill sits beside the rating pill and reads "כל הז׳אנרים" until a genre is chosen. It combines with status, rating and search, choosing "כל הז׳אנרים" clears it, and only genres that some title in this list has are offered. A chosen rating or genre that no title in the list has any more is dropped, so the list can't stay filtered with no way to clear it. This is the same rule as restaurant-visits 48. Why: issue #21 (added 2026-11-15, issue #21, extends 16 and 17).
76. **Settings fills in genres for TMDB titles that have none.** While such titles exist, Settings shows a "ז׳אנרים" card between the backup and feedback cards, with the action "השלמת ז׳אנרים מ-TMDB" and a line saying how many titles are waiting. While it runs, the action gives way to "משלים… N מתוך M" over a progress bar. When it ends, the line says how many were filled and how many failed, and the action becomes a retry for the failed ones. The card stays with its result until Settings is left, also when nothing is waiting any more, and after that it shows only while titles wait. One tap looks each one up through the details lookup (67) and saves its genres. A title tagged on the form meanwhile keeps its own tags. A title that TMDB gives no genres from the app's list for is saved with none, so it isn't offered again, and it counts as filled in the result line. Failed lookups are counted, left as they were, and offered again. When every lookup fails, the line says only how many failed. The result shows only while the titles still waiting are the failed ones. If more titles start waiting, for example after a restore, the card goes back to the count and the first-run action. A run left running when Settings is closed still saves each title, but its result isn't shown on return. Why: without it, the genre filter misses every title logged before genres existed (issue #21). Decided by the owner on 2026-11-15 (added 2026-11-15, issue #21, extends 21 and 74).
78. **iOS's own edge swipe is blocked, so swipe-back is the app's alone.** A touch that starts within 20 px of either screen edge is cancelled before iOS can begin its back swipe, except on tap targets (buttons, links, fields and Ionic controls), which still respond at the edge. An alert's backdrop is not a tap target here, so an edge swipe can't move the page under an open alert, and a tap outside an alert in that strip doesn't close it, as with iOS's own alerts. Ionic's swipe-back, which starts within 50 px of the right edge, still runs, and the page follows the finger. A vertical scroll that starts in that 20 px strip outside a tap target doesn't scroll. Why: in the Home Screen app on a Hebrew iPhone, both gestures start at the right edge, and together they could go back twice, jump or freeze taps (issue #24). It follows prayer-log 84 and workout-log 34, but it checks the finger that just landed rather than the first one on the screen, and it drops their backdrop exception. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-16 (added 2026-11-16, issue #24).

### Data

22. **All entries live on the device in IndexedDB, with a JSON backup file.** There is no account. Why: this matches the owner's other apps and keeps the list private.
23. **The backup format is versioned, and new fields must be optional** so old backups still import. Restoring replaces the whole list after confirmation.
24. **The list works offline; adding by search needs a connection.** When search fails, the add screen says so and offers manual entry.
37. **A failed save or delete says so.** A short message appears and the form stays open with what was typed, so it can be tried again. Why: otherwise the button appears to do nothing (added 2026-11-07, extends 22).
45. **Restored entries are normalised like saved ones.** Why: a hand-edited or older file can't store fields decision 5 rules out (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 5 and 23).
46. **A backup is restored whole or not at all.** An invalid or newer file leaves the list untouched and says so. Why: a partial restore would quietly lose titles (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 23).
93. **Any change to the backup format that an older app can't read raises the backup version:** a new field, or a new allowed type, status, rating, genre or source. The app imports every version up to its own. A file from a newer version is refused with "הגיבוי מגרסה חדשה יותר של האפליקציה. צריך לעדכן אותה ולנסות שוב.", not with "קובץ הגיבוי לא תקין", even if the rest of it can't be read, because updating is the first thing to try. A version is a whole number from 1 up: anything else, such as 3.5 or "3" written as text, makes the file invalid even when it's higher than the app's own, since the app only writes whole versions and updating wouldn't help. Version 2 (2026-11-26) covers genres, end year, hours and the ongoing status, which were added under version 1. A unit test pins the fields and allowed values to the version, so a change without a bump fails the build. Apps built before version 2 call a version-2 file invalid, and lose nothing. Why: an older app drops fields it doesn't know, so a phone still running a cached version restored a newer backup and quietly lost them. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-26 (added 2026-11-26, issue #36, clarifies 23 and 46).
94. **A backup where two titles share an id is invalid** and isn't restored. Why: restore writes each title by its id, so one of the two would vanish while the restore reported success. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-26 (added 2026-11-26, issue #32, clarifies 46).
47. **The future-date rule applies to the form only; restore keeps dates as they are.** Why: a restore must never alter history (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 8 and 23).
95. **On the form, the future-date check applies only when the date is changed.** An entry saved with a date or year after today, such as one restored from a backup (47), can be edited and saved with that date kept. Changing it to another date after today is blocked as before, and changing it back to the saved date is allowed again. The date picker and the year's + still stop at today (61, the year stepper), so the saved date comes back by typing the year or by cancelling the edit. Switching between an exact date and only a year counts as a change. Why: editing a restored title's rating or note mustn't force its history to change. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-22 (added 2026-11-26, issue #37, clarifies 8 and 47).
48. **Search with no results and at least one source failing says search is unavailable,** not that nothing was found. Why: the owner shouldn't conclude a title doesn't exist (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 24).

### Search service and third-party terms

25. **A small Cloudflare Worker holds the API keys and combines the sources:** `GET /search?q=...` returns `{ results, unavailable }`. One failing source doesn't fail the search; it's listed in `unavailable`. Why: GitHub Pages is static and the repo is public, so keys can't live in the client.
26. **The Worker only serves the app's own origins.** Why: a basic guard against other sites using the keys. Cloudflare's example CORS code isn't copied as is, because it reflects the Worker's own origin and lets callers choose the upstream.
27. **Posters are loaded from the source and never saved in the database or backup.** The entry stores the poster's URL. The service worker may cache images for up to 30 days. Offline, a poster that isn't cached shows a coloured placeholder with the title. Why: TMDB's terms limit caching to 6 months. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-06. Title and year are stored as the owner's own record, and whether TMDB's limit reaches those is unclear.
28. **Settings show TMDB's logo and the notice its terms require:** "This application uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB."
29. **IGDB's terms still need reading before game search is switched on.** The Worker skips IGDB until its credentials exist. Open gap: `api-docs.igdb.com` refused automated reading on 2026-11-06 (superseded by 78 and 79).
78. **IGDB's terms allow this app, so game search is on.** The IGDB API docs, read in a browser on 2026-11-16, say "The IGDB.com API is free for non-commercial usage under the terms of the Twitch Developer Service Agreement". Their FAQ says "we prefer if you store and serve the data", and asks for attribution only as part of a commercial partnership, so the app shows no IGDB notice. The credentials are a Twitch app's `TWITCH_CLIENT_ID` and `TWITCH_CLIENT_SECRET`, set in the Cloudflare dashboard like `TMDB_TOKEN` (56). Why: games never appeared in search, because the credentials were never set (issue #20) (added 2026-11-16, issue #20, closes the gap in 29).
79. **Missing or empty IGDB credentials list IGDB in `unavailable`,** as a missing TMDB token does, instead of skipping it silently. Why: the silent skip hid for ten days that game search never ran (added 2026-11-16, issue #20, replaces the skip in 29).
83. **IGDB search leaves out editions,** such as a Collector's Edition, using IGDB's own `where version_parent = null`. DLC and expansions stay, since one can be finished on its own. Why: an edition repeats its game under a longer name (added 2026-11-16, issue #20, extends 11).

35. **Each search source has an 8-second deadline.** A source that misses it is listed in `unavailable`, and the rest still return. Why: one hung source must not hang the whole search (added 2026-11-07, extends 25).
49. **A request with no `Origin` header is refused too** (added 2026-11-07, clarifies 26).
54. **The search service's address is committed in `.env.production`.** Why: GitHub Pages builds the app from the repo, so an address kept only on the laptop leaves search unavailable on the phone. The address isn't secret, because the TMDB token stays in the Worker (added 2026-11-08, clarifies 25).
55. **The same address is also committed in `.env.development`,** so `npm run dev` on port 5173 searches for real. Tests run in Vitest's `test` mode, which reads neither file, and set the address themselves. Why: the Worker already allows `http://localhost:5173`, and without the file local search says it's unavailable (added 2026-11-09, extends 54).
56. **A push to `main` that changes `worker/` deploys the Worker,** after its tests and type check pass, through the "Deploy search Worker" workflow. It uses a Cloudflare API token limited to editing Workers, saved as the repo secret `CLOUDFLARE_API_TOKEN`, with `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` beside it. The Worker's own secrets, such as `TMDB_TOKEN`, are set in the Cloudflare dashboard and survive deploys. Why: otherwise a merged search fix passes every check while the phone keeps using the old search service. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-09 (added 2026-11-09, extends 25).
57. **The app skips search results whose type or source it doesn't know, and shows the rest.** Any other malformed result, such as a poster address that isn't a URL, still fails the whole search, so bad data never reaches a backup. Why: the Worker deploys on merge, but the phone may run an older app until it reloads, and one new kind of result must not turn every search into "unavailable". Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (added 2026-11-10, extends 25 and 56).
58. **When the app skips every result under decision 57, it says nothing was found,** as when nothing matches. Why: this only happens on an outdated app, which updates on the next reload, and manual entry stays available. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (added 2026-11-10, clarifies 48 and 57).
59. **The Origin check is a courtesy guard, not access control.** Any script can send a faked `Origin`, and the Worker's address is public, so others could send searches through it. They can't read the TMDB token, but they would spend its allowance and the Cloudflare request quota. On the free Workers plan, requests past the daily limit fail rather than being billed. If abuse shows up (TMDB rejects the token, or Cloudflare usage jumps), add a per-IP rate limit to the Worker and replace the token in the Cloudflare dashboard. Why: for a single-user app, the risk doesn't justify more code now. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-10 (added 2026-11-10, clarifies 26 and 49).
62. **The Worker also answers `GET /seasons?tv=<id>` with `{ seasons }`, the aired season count of decision 61,** from TMDB's TV details. It counts the `seasons` items numbered 1 or more whose air date is a full date on or before today (UTC). It doesn't use `number_of_seasons`, since TMDB doesn't document whether that counts announced seasons. An id that isn't all digits gets 400, and a TMDB failure or a missing token gets 502. It sits behind the same Origin check (26, 59), with no caching and no deadline, and the app cancels the call when the sheet closes (added 2026-11-12, extends 25).
65. **`/seasons` also returns `endYear` when TMDB's status is "Ended" or "Canceled",** taken from `last_air_date`. Any other status, or a missing date, leaves the field out. The app treats it as optional, so an older app ignores it, and a reply without it still fills in seasons (added 2026-11-13, issue #16, extends 57, 62 on `/seasons`, and 64).
67. **The Worker answers `GET /details?title=<tv|movie>/<id>` with the facts of decision 66.** It makes three TMDB calls in parallel:
    - details;
    - credits: `aggregate_credits` for a series, `credits` for a movie;
    - watch providers, keeping the `IL` region's `flatrate` services in TMDB's display order.

    It doesn't use `append_to_response`, since TMDB doesn't document the keys appended data arrives under. A title that isn't `tv/` or `movie/` with digits gets 400, and any TMDB failure or a missing token gets 502. It sits behind the same Origin check (26, 59), with no caching and no deadline, and the app cancels the call when the page closes (added 2026-11-13, issue #17, extends 25 and 62 on `/seasons`).
77. **TMDB search results also carry `genreIds`, TMDB's genre numbers, which the app maps to its own genres (74).** The field is optional, so an older app ignores it, and an older Worker leaves a new pick's genres unset (added 2026-11-15, issue #21, extends 25 and 57).
100. **Dependabot opens weekly update PRs for the app's npm packages, the Worker's own lockfile in `worker/` and the pinned GitHub Actions, each after a 7-day cooldown.** Minor and patch updates arrive as one grouped PR per directory, and a major arrives on its own. Majors of `react-router` and `react-router-dom` are ignored, because `@ionic/react-router` 9 needs `react-router` below 7. Majors of `@types/node` are ignored too, because it follows the Node 24 that CI runs. The "Dependabot security updates" setting stays off, as in the sibling apps, so a fix arrives with the weekly PRs. Dependabot alerts, the PR dependency review and a Trivy workflow flag a vulnerability sooner. Trivy runs on every PR, on every push to `main` and each Monday, and fails on a fixable HIGH or CRITICAL finding. Merging a Worker update redeploys the Worker through 56. Why: with no config, the pinned actions and both lockfiles never got update PRs, security fixes included. The config and the Trivy workflow are copied from the sibling apps. Decided by the owner on 2026-11-26 (added 2026-11-26, issue #35, extends 56).
